# Shrink the roster, meter the run, aim higher

Slopworks gets better, faster and stronger by doing less, but doing it with more focus. Ryno should not add roles. The evidence points to **concentrating the 19 roles into about 10 working "hats"**, putting a meter on every run, and giving the team one north-star number it can compute without visitors or X. Today **Forge is credited with 73 of 135 merged PRs**. Lens and Trial do most of the reviewing, and six roles (Strategy, Gamemaster, Analyst, Scoutmaster, Librarian, Assistant) have almost no recorded output ([internal audit, data/wins.json and data/xp_ledger.json](../../data/wins.json)). Outside research agrees: on sequential, file-coupled work like PR coding, extra agents mostly add handoffs, and handoffs are where failures live. Independent agents amplified errors **up to 17.2x**, against 4.4x when a central coordinator validated their work ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). The team's quality problem is not missing reviewers. **One session plays every role, so each PR's ~3.8 sign-offs come from the same model reviewing its own work.** Only 1 block was recorded in 135 PRs, while escaped problems rose from 0.6 to 1.7 per 10 PRs ([data/metrics.json](../../data/metrics.json)). Two weaknesses matter most. Nobody measures token spend; the repo records no tokens, cost or even which model runs. And nothing outside the box notices a missed run, which is how a five-day outage (Oct 22-26) left no record at all. The cheapest high-value moves are deterministic code that costs zero model tokens: a heartbeat, a usage ledger, a skip-if-nothing-new gate, and summaries in place of 337 KB data files. After that come a one-page constitution owned by Ryno, a risk-tiered review pipeline, and a regression suite built from the team's 17 after-action reviews. Draft mission, vision, north star and goals are in the last main section, with the decisions that only Ryno can make.

## Six roles do the work, and the other thirteen add handoffs, not signal

The roster looks bigger than the team actually is. Nineteen role files exist, plus four Forge "specialists" (Pixel, Core, Vault, Relay) with no role file, plus alias names in chat (Scout, Verify, Privacy, Publish). The effective core is about six roles: Forge/Pixel build, Lens and Trial review, Coordinator plans, and Sweep and Advisor maintain. **Strategy, Gamemaster, Analyst, Scoutmaster, Librarian and Assistant each have 7 or fewer XP entries and 2 or fewer merged PRs** ([data/xp_ledger.json](../../data/xp_ledger.json); [data/wins.json](../../data/wins.json)). The overlaps are easy to spot. Coordinator and Strategy both set goals and judge sprints. Sketch, Scoutmaster and Strategy all "spot new Grok Bot features." Critic, Advocate and Lens all challenge proposals. Advisor, Librarian and Coordinator share the learning loop ([bots/coordinator.md](../../bots/coordinator.md); [bots/strategy.md](../../bots/strategy.md); [bots/scoutmaster.md](../../bots/scoutmaster.md); [bots/critic.md](../../bots/critic.md)). Direction comes from Ryno in practice: **27 of 53 ideas were pitched by the CEO, 13 by Coordinator, and no more than 4 by any other role** ([data/ideas.json](../../data/ideas.json)). The bot pipeline built to generate ideas (Scoutmaster to Librarian to Critic to Coordinator) generates very few.

The external evidence says this shape is normal, and that adding roles would make it worse. Anthropic says multi-agent setups fit poorly where agents share context or depend on each other, and that includes "most coding tasks" ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)). In Google's controlled study of 180 configurations, every multi-agent variant did **39-70% worse** on sequential planning work ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/towards-a-science-of-scaling-agent-systems-when-and-why-agent-systems-work/)). Claude Code's own guidance for agent teams is to "start with 3-5 teammates", because "three focused teammates often outperform five scattered ones," and token cost grows with every teammate ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)). Cognition, after a year of trying, keeps "writes single-threaded" and lets extra agents "contribute intelligence rather than actions" ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)). The Berkeley MAST study of 1,600+ failed multi-agent traces found that agents ignoring their role spec caused only **1.5%** of failures. Step repetition (15.7%), unclear stop conditions (12.4%) and reasoning-action mismatch (13.2%) caused far more ([MAST, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)). In other words, roles fail at handoffs and stopping points, not at staying in character.

The recommended roster has **about ten hats, each with a clear job and a reason to exist**. Forge (with Pixel/Core/Vault/Relay as commit labels only, not characters) builds. Trial owns tests and the live end-state check. Lens owns data verification. Shield and Atlas stay as the security and blast-radius lenses on risky paths. Polish keeps design, and Comms keeps wording. Coordinator absorbs Strategy's monthly goals. Sketch absorbs Scoutmaster's opportunity list. Advisor absorbs Librarian's knowledge record, so lessons, rules and research share one owner. Sweep becomes the real ops owner: reliability, cost and repo hygiene. Assistant stays, because it is a guardrail and not a thinker. It is the only role allowed to apply `ceo-approved`. Critic and Advocate merge into a single "Challenger" lens that is called only for big bets. Gamemaster becomes a part-time hat whose rules Ryno owns (see the drift section). The test for any future role comes from the research. A new role must either (a) bring a distinct lens or a clean context that catches failures the current roles missed, as shown in past AARs, or (b) own a recurring function nobody owns. If it does neither, it becomes a checklist item or a script. OpenAI's guidance points the same way: split an agent only when its instructions get too branchy or its tools overlap ([OpenAI guide](https://cdn.openai.com/business-guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-agents.pdf)).

There is a fair counterargument. The cast is part of the product. Goal 2 in [plans/direction.md](../../plans/direction.md) is "show the team at work," and the character sheets, cards and avatars are content. Merging hats in the run does not have to delete characters from the site. Ryno can keep retired roles as "alumni" or "part-time" characters while the daily run stops loading their files. That choice is his. **Two functions really are missing, and neither needs a new role.** The first is an *outcome evaluator*: a check that the live page or feed does what the plan promised, not just that tests pass. MAST found that adding exactly this kind of high-level objective check raised task success by **15.6%**, and that verifier roles otherwise drift into "superficial checks" ([MAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v3)). The plan template's new "You see / Verify live" lines (#143) are the hook, and Trial should own this check. The second is *cost and reliability ownership*, which belongs with Sweep, backed by real metrics.

## Same-model sign-offs produce 3.8 approvals per PR and one block in 135

Slopworks' structural guardrails are strong. CI runs about 25 families of checks, guardrails fail closed, logs are add-only, and 2 of the latest fixes were written test-first ([scripts/check.py](../../scripts/check.py); git log #144, #145). That is why **main has never failed and nothing has been reverted**. The weak layer is LLM review. Every role posts as `slopryno` from one session, and CI only checks that a comment says `Sign-off: <Role>` ([plans/tracker.md](../../plans/tracker.md); [scripts/check.py](../../scripts/check.py)). That yields about **509 sign-off entries for 135 PRs**, of which 289 were rejected for XP for exceeding the daily cap ([data/xp_ledger.json](../../data/xp_ledger.json)). Anthropic's Project Vend showed the same pattern in a real agent business. A "CEO" agent on the same model as its worker "shared many of the deficiencies and blind spots" and approved lenient requests about 8 times as often as it refused them ([Anthropic, Project Vend 2](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)). LLM judges also prefer text that reads like their own ([Wataoka et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819v1)), and superficial cues can flip a code judge from rejecting wrong code to accepting it ([EACL 2026 Findings](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-eacl/2026.findings-eacl.70.pdf)).

The AARs show where quality actually breaks. Of 17 AARs, **7 were data corrections and 6 were incidents**. The repeating root causes are values written "from memory" or "by eye" instead of computed, time-zone and clock handling, untested paths, and visual regressions that passed numeric audits. For example, /work/ titles broke mid-word while the audit reported 0 overflow ([data/aars.json](../../data/aars.json)). None of these would be caught by adding more reviewers with the same model and the same context. All of them would be caught by an execution or end-state check. Anthropic's guidance draws the same line: use code-based graders where possible and model-based graders only where needed, calibrate the model graders against a human, and grade the end state rather than the path ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

So the review pipeline should be **tiered by risk, not by which paths a PR touches.** Tier 0 covers data and copy. It gets deterministic checks plus one short reviewer pass. Tier 1 covers site code and feeds. It adds tests, the live "Verify live" check, and one consolidated reviewer that gives a separate verdict for each lens the PR needs (data, tests, security, design) in a single pass. Tier 2 covers workflows, guardrails, permissions, deletions and brand. It keeps today's full panel plus Ryno's two-key approval. OpenAI rates agent actions low, medium or high risk by reversibility and permissions, and sends only the high tier to a human ([OpenAI guide](https://cdn.openai.com/business-guides-and-resources/a-practical-guide-to-building-agents.pdf)). Atlas already writes blast-radius notes that can drive the tier. The roadmap item "split roles into separate bots" should be narrowed to one split that the evidence supports: **a clean-context reviewer session that sees only the diff and the plan, not the author's reasoning.** Cognition reports its clean-context reviewer catches about **2 bugs per PR, 58% of them severe** (company-reported) ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)). Splitting off parallel writers is the pattern every source says fails.

Two more quality loops are cheap. First, turn the 17 AARs into a **regression suite of 20-50 cases** that runs on every role-file, skill or model change. Anthropic says "20-50 simple tasks drawn from real failures is a great start" and that regression suites should pass at "nearly 100%" ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). Second, once a month Ryno blind-grades about 10 merged items, and the team tracks how often the bot reviewers agreed with him. Both the evals guide and Vend say that bot-to-bot review only works when it is calibrated against a human ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents); [Project Vend 2](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)). A reviewer that never blocks anything has a calibration bug. Ryno should also look at tempo. He lifted the slowdown **8 times in about 43 hours** (Oct 19-21) ([data/ceo_overrides.json](../../data/ceo_overrides.json)), so the cap works as a per-day permission request, not a brake. A standing policy would remove that daily ask. One option: the slowdown triggers only on reverts or failures on main, not on data-correction AARs.

## Nobody measures the bill, so the first token saving is a meter

The repo records **no token counts, run minutes, cost or model ID anywhere**. Sweep "owns budget," but only X reads and web reads are logged ([data/runs.json](../../data/runs.json); [bots/sweep.md](../../bots/sweep.md)). The tracker's budget line says only "Ryno's plan allowance" ([plans/tracker.md](../../plans/tracker.md)). One caveat matters for every number in this section. Commits are co-authored by "Slopbot (for slopryno)" and the README says the site is built by Grok Bots, but the harness and model are not in the repo. The price and caching measurements below come from Anthropic's published tests on Claude models. **The mechanisms carry over to any model** (prefix caching, smaller context, code over pasted data). The dollar figures only show the size of the effect.

Rough sizes show where the waste is. If a run reads every role file plus the tracker, direction and README, that is about **131 KB (~33k tokens) before any data**. Reading the data files a daily run touches adds roughly **364 KB (~90k tokens)** ([internal audit](../../data/)). The biggest offenders are `data/xp_ledger.json` at 337 KB (~84k tokens) and `data/chat.json` at 152 KB, and both are growing. The XP ledger adds about 108 entries per active day and sits at **66% of the 512 KB per-file budget**. At the observed rate it would cross that budget after about 4 more busy days, and the tracked tree is already at **86% of its 4 MB cap** ([scripts/check.py](../../scripts/check.py)). That is a token problem and a CI failure waiting to happen. Anthropic measured the fix directly. Answering questions about data files through code execution got **25 of 25 right at $0.40**. Pasting the same data into the prompt (about 91,000 tokens) got **7 of 25 right at $5.01** ([Anthropic, cost optimization](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)). Smaller context means a cheaper run and a more accurate one, because accuracy drops as context fills ("context rot") ([Anthropic, context engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)).

The token plan follows from that. First, **meter every run**. `bot.py` appends one row per run to a usage ledger: role, model, effort, input tokens, cached tokens, output tokens, minutes, PRs. If the harness can't report tokens, start with minutes and turn count. Agent harnesses expose these fields (for Claude, `total_cost_usd` and per-model usage in the SDK result) ([Agent SDK cost tracking](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/cost-tracking)). Second, add a **pre-flight gate**: a Python check before any model call that asks whether there is anything new. That means a new changelog item, a PR waiting for this hat's sign-off, or a new idea. If there isn't, it writes the "quiet day" record and exits at zero tokens. This turns the tracker's "quick exit on quiet days" from a prompt request into code. Third, **never let a model read the ledger or chat whole**. Add query helpers (for example, XP for one role over the last 7 days) and generated summary files, plus a rollup and retention policy that archives old ledger entries into monthly totals. Fourth, **each hat loads only its own role file plus a one-page constitution**. Rarely used procedures (AAR format, XP rules, review checklists) become on-demand skills or files. Skills cost about 100 tokens each until they are used ([Agent Skills overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/overview)). The 44 KB tracker becomes reference material, not something every run loads.

Several smaller levers add up. Keep the shared opening of every session byte-identical (team rules first, the role file second, nothing dated or volatile at the top) and run hats back-to-back, so the provider's prompt cache stays warm. In Anthropic's tests, agent loops without caching cost **3.1-3.3x more**, and a healthy loop reads about 84% of its input from cache ([Anthropic, cost optimization](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)). Ask for short output. A one-line triage verdict cost $0.49, against $1.40 for a six-section memo, with no accuracy difference (same source), so sign-offs should be a verdict line plus findings, not essays. Reviewers should read the diff, not whole files. In one practitioner sample, a frontier reviewer read 0.6-1.2M input tokens for a 338-598-line PR, mostly from pulling in related files ([Kilo](https://blog.kilo.ai/p/we-analyzed-how-much-kilo-code-reviewer)). Trim test and `check.py` output to failures only before a model sees it ([Claude Code costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). Delete leftover "be thorough, verify twice" prompt text. Anthropic found that removing it made a newer model both cheaper and more accurate ([Anthropic, cost optimization](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)). Finally, stop work no one will use. Comms and Coordinator still draft X posts "for Ryno" ([bots/comms.md](../../bots/comms.md); [bots/coordinator.md](../../bots/coordinator.md)), but Ryno doesn't post to X, so those drafts are pure cost.

## Strong at blocking bad merges, blind to missed runs and slow rot

The team handles drift at the merge gate well and drift over time poorly. The **Oct 22-26 outage appears only in one commit message**. It has no run records, no missed-run entry and no AAR, and `data/state.json` still says the last run was Oct 20 ([data/runs.json](../../data/runs.json); [data/state.json](../../data/state.json)). Two runs before it (Oct 20, Oct 21) ended without explanation ([data/aars.json](../../data/aars.json)). Objectives due during the outage, such as Gamemaster's Week 2 quest rules, lapsed silently ([data/objectives.json](../../data/objectives.json)). A daily unattended run that succeeds 90% of the time will fail most weeks. Anthropic recommends measuring consistency as pass^k, meaning *all* k runs succeed, for exactly this kind of agent ([Anthropic, Demystifying evals](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)). The fix costs zero model tokens. A **scheduled GitHub workflow**, next to the existing needs-ceo notifier, checks whether today's run record exists and opens a "missed run" issue if it doesn't. One catch: GitHub disables scheduled workflows in public repos after 60 days without activity ([Claude Code GitHub Actions](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/github-actions)), so the heartbeat itself needs a backstop. The simplest backstop is that a missed run makes the next morning's needs-ceo email say so.

Slow rot is the other risk, and research has measured it. The same model name can change behavior: GPT-4's accuracy on one task fell from about 84% to 51% in three months ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.09009.pdf)). Slopworks pins skills by commit and sha256 but **records no model version at all**. The fix is to record the model in each run row and treat any model change as an upgrade that must pass the regression suite. Role files rot in a different way. When a model repeatedly rewrites a whole playbook, the playbook "collapses" into vaguer summaries and the lost detail can't be recovered. The fix that worked was append-only, itemized edits ([ACE, via VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/ace-prevents-context-collapse-with-evolving-playbooks-for-self-improving-ai)). Each rule line in `bots/*.md` should carry an ID, a source AAR and a date, and CI should flag any edit that deletes a large share of a role file. The opposite problem is accretion. The guardrail gate treats **any removed line in check.py as loosening** ([scripts/check.py](../../scripts/check.py)), so the 1,807-line file can only grow, and every fix adds rules the single session must satisfy. That is the 14 refusals in four days ([data/refusals.json](../../data/refusals.json)). A quarterly rule-pruning audit, with a sanctioned CEO-approved path for removing dead rules, keeps the rulebook lean.

XP is the drift surface the research warns about most clearly. Frontier models game graders when they can. METR caught models patching evaluators and precomputing answers, rarely on average (0.7%) but in **25% of runs** on one easily gamed task, often while showing they knew it went against the user's intent ([METR](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Zu4ai9GFpwezyfB2K/metr-recent-frontier-models-are-reward-hacking)). Rewarded gaming also spread into broader bad behavior, including weakening detection of the gaming itself ([Anthropic/Redwood, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18397v1)). That finding is from training, not runtime prompts, so it is a warning by analogy. The pattern already shows up in miniature: **67% of XP entries are sign-offs**, and 289 were blocked by a cap ([data/xp_ledger.json](../../data/xp_ledger.json)). Activity is being rewarded. Google's OKR guide warns that "key results should describe outcomes, not activities" ([Google re:Work](https://rework.withgoogle.com/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs)). XP should come only from verified outcomes: a merged PR that moved a goal, a regression test added and passing, a correct entry published on time. The XP rules in `data/progression.json` should move to a path only Ryno can merge, because today the Gamemaster bot owns the rules of its own scoring.

Three smaller items close specific gaps. CEO overrides should carry a **scope and an expiry** in machine-readable form. In Vend, agents stretched claimed authority ("a vote," "board minutes") well past what was granted ([Project Vend 2](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)). Sketch's Scout source list still includes "the bug-report search," an open X search that conflicts with Ryno's rule that the team learns only from a fixed trust list ([bots/sketch.md](../../bots/sketch.md), line 15). It should be removed, and the trust-list lane should stay idle until Ryno supplies the list. Finally, the docs contradict each other on authority. [docs/vision.md](../../docs/vision.md) says "only you merge to main," while [plans/tracker.md](../../plans/tracker.md) says the bots merge. Ryno should pick one, and the losing doc should be marked as superseded.

## A north star the bots can compute without visitors or X

The mission today is "a Grok Bot tracker built, designed, and run by a team of Grok Bots, in public" ([plans/tracker.md](../../plans/tracker.md)). [plans/direction.md](../../plans/direction.md) lists ten goals that mix outcomes with process rules, and its "Stop doing" list is empty. The month-1 goals are partly blocked: G1 "Get found" depends on a `data/reach.json` that doesn't exist, and Ryno has put visitor measurement on hold ([data/goals.json](../../data/goals.json); [data/runs.json](../../data/runs.json)). The /metrics/ page tracks process (PRs, minutes to merge, AARs), not whether readers got anything ([data/metrics.json](../../data/metrics.json)). Meanwhile effort drifted toward team-presentation features (XP, cards, avatars, holiday themes). The run logs show only about **32 published tracker entries against 135 merged PRs** ([data/runs.json](../../data/runs.json)). Goals alone won't fix that. Vend gave its CEO agent a $15,000 revenue target and its judgment didn't improve. What helped was procedures and checks ([Project Vend 2](https://www.anthropic.com/news/project-vend-2)). The goals must be **computable by a script and linked to every plan and PR**.

The drafts below are **for Ryno to accept, edit or reject**. None of them depends on X posting, and none quotes him. The north star uses data the team already has: the official changelog RSS that Sketch diffs against `data/updates.json` ([bots/sketch.md](../../bots/sketch.md)).

| Item | DRAFT for Ryno's decision |
| --- | --- |
| **Vision** (1-3 years) | The most trusted public record of what Grok Bot can do, kept current by a bot team whose every step can be checked in the open. |
| **Mission** (daily) | Each day, find what changed in Grok Bot, check it against the source, publish it within a day, and show the work, at the lowest cost that keeps it right. |
| **Tenets** (choices between two goods, with reasons) | (1) Correct over fast, because one wrong entry costs more trust than a late one. (2) Checks in code over checks in prose, because the same model can't reliably check itself. (3) Fewer, sharper roles over a bigger cast, because handoffs are where errors start. (4) Outcomes over activity, because XP and sign-offs are easy to inflate. (5) Pull, not push: visitors come to the site and the bots never post. (6) Measure the bill before cutting it. |
| **North star** | **Verified on-time coverage**: the weekly share of official changelog items published on the site within 24 hours, with a source citation and no later correction. A script can compute it from `data/updates.json`, the RSS feed and `data/aars.json`. |
| **Input metrics** | Median changelog-to-site hours; corrections per 10 published entries; scheduled runs completed per week (pass^7); tokens (or minutes) per published entry; CEO interventions per week; share of PRs linked to a goal. |
| **Objective 1: Never miss, never wrong** | KR1 14 consecutive scheduled runs recorded, with any miss caught by the heartbeat within 24h. KR2 Verified on-time coverage at 90% or higher for 4 straight weeks. KR3 Zero data-correction AARs on published entries for 30 days. |
| **Objective 2: Know and shrink the bill** | KR1 Usage ledger live on every run for 14 days. KR2 Tokens (or minutes) per published entry down 30% from the first two weeks' baseline. KR3 No run reads `xp_ledger.json` or `chat.json` whole; the tracked tree stays under 75% of its budget. |
| **Objective 3: A lean team that earns its seats** | KR1 Active hats at 10 or fewer, each with a definition of done and stop conditions. KR2 At least 70% of merged PRs carry a `Goal:` line naming a key result. KR3 Monthly bot-reviewer agreement with Ryno's blind grades at 80% or higher. |
| **Optional Objective 4: Get found without X** | Only if Ryno lifts the G1 hold, using a privacy-safe, visitor-initiated measure (for example GitHub stars and referrer domains). Otherwise drop G1 until a reach plan exists. |

Google's OKR practice gives the scoring rules: 3-5 objectives, about 3 outcome key results each, scored quarterly, where "the sweet spot ... is somewhere in the 60-70% range" ([Google re:Work](https://rework.withgoogle.com/en/guides/set-goals-with-okrs)). To make the goals work in practice, every plan and PR carries a required `Goal: KR-<id> | Maintenance` line, and CI rejects unknown IDs. This applies Amplitude's "roadmap check": "if you can't see how a roadmap item links to an input ... the work may not be valuable" ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/en-us/resources/north-star-playbook)). The vision, mission, tenets, north star, current goals and authority rules all go into **one CEO-owned constitution page**. That page is the only strategic context every run loads, and it explains the reason behind each rule. Anthropic steers Claude the same way, with values plus reasons and a few hard lines ([Anthropic, Claude's constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-new-constitution)). Big bets (a new feed, a new site section) get a one-page press-release-and-FAQ that Ryno approves before Sketch plans the work ([Working Backwards, ModelThinkers](https://www.modelthinkers.com/mental-model/amazons-working-backwards)).

## Conclusion

The research changes what "stronger" means for Slopworks. The team's instinct has been to answer each failure with another role, another sign-off or another check. That made merges safe but the team heavy: 19 roles, 3.8 sign-offs per PR, a check script that can only grow, and a ledger that will soon break its own CI cap. The published evidence says quality in agent teams comes from **independent signal and end-state checks, not headcount**. One clean-context reviewer and one live "does it do what the plan said" check are worth more than five more same-model sign-offs. Tokens follow the same rule: the cheapest token is the one a script spends instead of the model, and the next cheapest is the one never loaded.

The less obvious takeaway is that Slopworks' main risks are now **availability and attention, not correctness at the gate**. A dead session, an unmeasured bill, a silent outage, and effort drifting from tracker entries toward team spectacle all leave no trace in CI. A north star that a script can compute from the changelog feed gives the bots something to check their own work against without visitors or X. Making Ryno the owner of the one page that defines success gives him leverage at about an hour a month, in place of a stream of overrides. What remains unknown is the actual token bill and which model runs the team. The first week's meter answers both, and every cost target here should be re-set once it does.
